\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph*{What the results mean}
In this simulation, a behavioural signal that enters the offloading decision as a weighted term raises deadline success over the same rule without trust at every degraded fraction tested, and the node-local head comes within $0.001$--$0.003$ of an oracle that reads the sealed fault state (Sec.~\ref{sec:behav}).
% src: A5, A6
Most of that gain is already available from the node's own evidence: a non-learned monitor, M, which uses only the RSU's own \texttt{success\_ewma}, recovers most of the trust-aware gain, and the pre-registered rule did not separate it from the learned head (Sec.~\ref{sec:learning}).
% src: A27, A23 (X18: we do not say that learning adds nothing; the label is NULL under a 0.01 floor with signed differences 0.003-0.005)
We read this as a statement about the floor we fixed in advance and about this scenario, not as a statement that the learned head is unnecessary.
% src: X18

On graph structure, the load-bearing hypothesis was tested four times and is not supported.
The graph head was worse than the node-local head on the pre-registered diagnostic (NEGATIVE) and under staggered onset, where a neighbour lead exists (NEGATIVE); it was not better at any closed-loop point (NEGATIVE at 20\% and 30\% degraded); and a contained graph head that starts as C was not distinguishable from it (NULL), including when both heads were trained on a forward-looking objective (NULL).
For the shared-onset scenario we also measured a boundary condition: own-node evidence is never later than neighbour evidence there, so a lead-time advantage for message passing is not available by construction (Secs.~\ref{sec:graph} and~\ref{sec:objective}).
% src: A9, A10, A11, A25, A28, A29; X1, X2, X17, X21, X23 (no generalisation: one injection model, S = 300, one scenario per seed, open-loop-trained heads, one D2 construction, one horizon objective)
Two further scenarios designed to favour graph structure, a fault spreading at a known lag and a cold start, also did not favour it: Sa was NOT SUPPORTED, the control was VIOLATED with D later, and Sb was NOT SEPARATED (Sec.~\ref{sec:favour}).
% src: A31, A32; X25 (no mechanism; D's lower false-alarm rate is not offered as an advantage)
These results are for one injection model, one stagger size, one scenario per seed, heads trained on open-loop dispatch, one contained-head construction and one forward-looking objective; we make no claim about other regimes, objectives or architectures.
% src: X17, X21, X23

On the training objective, the confirmatory test returned NOT CONFIRMED: in this simulation the forward-looking objective made the node-local head detect degraded RSUs earlier than the next-task objective (EARLIER), but more false alarms were not ruled out (NOT ACCEPTABLE), and closed-loop success did not differ ($-0.0008$, descriptive) (Sec.~\ref{sec:objective}).
% src: A30; X24 (EARLIER only beside NOT ACCEPTABLE and NOT CONFIRMED; no claim that the objective improves dispatch); X23 (no mechanism)
\latcaveat
% src: A30 (D78 shared-rule caveat, verbatim); A19

The weighted rule and a gate on the same head performed equivalently on task success (Sec.~\ref{sec:gate}).
The case for a weighted term is therefore a design argument: an exactly decomposable score and no empty-candidate-set failure mode, and not a measured gain.
% src: A15, X3
Layer 1 explains the score and is exact; it does not explain why a node's trust has its value.
Layer 2 attributes the trust head's output weakly, unstably and only asynchronously, and each Layer 2 explanation is one draw (Secs.~\ref{sec:layer1}, \ref{sec:layer2} and~\ref{sec:overhead}).
% src: A12, A13, A14; X15, X5

\paragraph*{Untested hypotheses}
The following were not run, except where an item says so, and none of them is a finding.
They are the open questions that the record leaves, stated as hypotheses.
% src: A20 as re-issued in S15b (D74), verbatim list; "None of these, other than the single D2 construction of A28 and the single horizon objective of A29, was run and none may be reported as a finding"
\begin{itemize}
\item An observation-count feature.
\item Other architectures.
\item Training the trust heads on closed-loop dispatch, and the shift between the open-loop training dispatch and the closed-loop evaluation dispatch as a possible cause of D trailing C.
\item The next-task objective was departed from once (Sec.~\ref{sec:objective}): a forward-looking objective did not let the one contained graph construction (D2-h) detect earlier than C-h; other horizons, thresholds, regression targets and architectures are untested.
\item Dilution of D's own-node signal by its aggregation over many neighbours: this was tested for one contained construction (D2, Sec.~\ref{sec:graph}), while dilution in general is untested.
\item Collusion conditions where a graph model might help. (Cold start, which an earlier version of this list included, was tested in one scenario: Sec.~\ref{sec:favour}.)
\item Gating a different head.
\end{itemize}
None of these, other than the single D2 construction and the single horizon objective, was run, and none is reported as a finding.
Staggered onset, which an earlier version of this list included, was tested (Sec.~\ref{sec:graph}).
